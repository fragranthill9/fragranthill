# Script and interview questions

For you to read, practise and keep in mind. The shooting plan is in its own tab; nothing here is production logistics.

## How to use this script

You are not a journalist interrogating them and you are not their PR agency. You are the person who explains China's physical AI to a Western audience that is curious and sceptical in equal measure. Every question below is written so the answer helps them and is still interesting if the answer is "no".

**Register.** Executive conversational. Short questions, plain words, no jargon unless they use it first. The best questions in The Circuit are under 15 words. Emily Chang's method, in order: place and origin first, then one yardstick question that pins the hype to something concrete, then the hard question asked kindly, then the future. Her known weakness is the missing follow-up; you win by asking the second question ("and then what happened?", "how do you know?").

**Three moves to keep in your pocket.**

1. Label, then ask (Chris Voss): "It sounds like the night of the final was the most stressful night of the year. Walk me through it." A label earns a story; a bare "was it stressful?" earns a yes.
2. Ask for the specific day, the specific race, the specific number (Terry Gross, Cal Fussman). "Tell me about the last time the link dropped mid-race" beats "how reliable is the system".
3. Make the comparison for them, then let them correct you. "In Las Vegas, Vay drives real cars this way at 30 km/h on public roads. What you are doing is faster and the crowd is watching. Is that harder or easier?" Correcting a Western assumption is the clip they will enjoy giving you.

**When you get a PR answer** ("we are committed to building a world-class ecosystem"), do not argue. Say "give me an example", wait, and if nothing comes, move to a smaller question: "what did you change after the last event?" Small questions get true answers.

**Chinese on camera.** Each question has a Chinese version. Ask in whichever language gets the better answer; the subtitle team can handle both. If the interviewee is more fluent in Chinese, ask in Chinese and let them go long. A fluent 90-second answer in Chinese cuts better than a careful 20-second answer in English.

**Do not ask.** Anything that invites them to criticise the government, name competitors who failed, or promise a date they have not announced internally. Frame risks as "what you tell your team" and "what keeps you up at night", not "is this a bubble".

**Your value, said in the first three minutes.** You run a Chinese-founded studio in Sweden with a Western audience, and you are a VP at a 3D camera company that sits inside the robotics supply chain. Offer them the thing they cannot buy: a credible explanation to the West, in the West's own idiom. Say it plainly: "I want this to be the film a European investor or an Ericsson engineer watches to understand what you have actually built, not the hype version."

## Cold open and narration

Read these aloud until they sound like you. Voice-over lines are short because the pictures carry the weight. Where a number is in brackets, replace it with the number the CTO gives you on camera; never read a number they have not said.

**Cold open, over black, then the latency counter on a cockpit screen.**

"Eighty milliseconds. That is how long it takes this car to tell its driver where it is. The driver is ten kilometres away."

Cut: the car at speed, a crash, the driver's hands not moving. Beat.

"He felt nothing. He saw everything. Welcome to a sport where the referee is physics."

Title.

**Stakes, over establishing shots of HURA PARK in Anting and the Shanghai skyline.**

"Every robot company in the world has the same unsolved problem. The machines are not ready to run themselves. So someone, somewhere, has to drive them, from far away, fast enough that nothing breaks. In Las Vegas a company called Vay does it with rental cars at city speed. In Shanghai, this company decided to do it at racing speed, in public, with a crowd watching. They call it HURA GP. I came to find out whether it is a sport, a technology demo, or the first look at how robots will actually be run."

**Piece to camera, standing beside a cockpit, before your lap.**

"I used to build the box that connects a car to the network. The T-box. It phones home, updates the software, unlocks the doors. What they have built is the same box pointed the other way: instead of the car talking to the cloud, a human in the cloud drives the car. The question is how many milliseconds that costs, and whether a human can live with it. I am about to find out."

Cue: drive the lap. Keep the counter on screen. If you crash, keep rolling; it is the best thing that can happen.

**Bridge into the founder sit-down, over archival of the March 2026 final beside the F1 circuit.**

"In March, during the Formula 1 weekend, they ran their first season final a few hundred metres from the real circuit. The cars were on this track. The drivers were ten kilometres away. \[The winner's name\] won by \[margin\], which is less than the time it takes the video to reach his screen."

**Bridge into the CTO, over the block diagram animating.**

"Here is the whole trick in one picture. Control goes out, video comes back, and the race lives in the round trip. The company says that round trip is under eighty milliseconds. I wanted to know where every one of them goes."

**Bridge into the broadcast lead, over the production gallery.**

"There are two kinds of video in this building. One has to arrive in milliseconds or the car crashes. The other can arrive seconds late and nobody minds. The first is engineering. The second is show business. Both have to work."

**Turn to robots, over the robot test area.**

"Then they showed me why the racing is only the beginning."

**The cross-border beat, if it happens, over a split screen: Sweden and Shanghai.**

"Shanghai to Stockholm and back is about a hundred and sixty milliseconds on today's internet. Twice what they quote for the track. Nobody had tried to race across it. So we did."

**Closing voice-over, over the robot run and the counter.**

"I came to Shanghai to see a race. What I found was a company using a sport to rehearse the next decade: humans, at a distance, driving machines that are not ready to drive themselves. The cars are the test. The robots are the business. And the clock, as always, is the only referee that cannot be argued with."

**Your line at the very end, to camera, dry.**

"For the record: I crashed. The delay was \[number\] milliseconds. The excuse was mine."

## Interview 1: the event director (赛事)

Goal: get the definition of the sport, the stakes, the fairness answer and the international hook. Fourteen questions, asked in this order; the first four are not optional.

1. **Origin, with a failure in it.** "Tell me about the first time someone drove one of your cars from another city. Where were you standing, and what went wrong?" 讲讲第一次有人从另一个城市远程开你们车的那天。你站在哪？出了什么问题？ Why: The Circuit opens on place and origin. The failure makes it a story, not a pitch.
2. **One sentence, two audiences.** "If I am a Formula 1 fan in London, what is HURA GP in one sentence? And if I am an esports fan in Seoul?" 如果我是伦敦的 F1 车迷，一句话说 HURA GP 是什么？如果我是首尔的电竞玩家呢？ Why: forces positioning. The two answers tell you what they really think they are.
3. **The numbers of the final.** "This final: how many drivers, how many cars, where are the drivers sitting, and how far is the farthest one from the track?" 这次总决赛：多少车手、多少辆车、车手坐在哪儿、最远的离赛道多远？ Why: narration needs numbers. Get them on camera in their voice.
4. **The crash.** "What happens when a car crashes? Who pays, who fixes it, and does the driver see it?" 车撞了怎么办？谁付钱、谁修、车手看得到吗？ Why: this is where "real" becomes concrete for a sim-racing audience.
5. **Fairness.** "Two drivers, one in Shanghai and one in Chengdu. The Chengdu driver has 40 milliseconds more delay. Is that fair? What do you do about it?" 一个车手在上海，一个在成都，成都的延迟多 40 毫秒。公平吗？你们怎么处理？ Why: the central sporting question. Whether they equalise latency or not is a key beat of the film.
6. **Category.** "Is this esports, motorsport, or something that does not have a name yet? Who do you want to sanction you, the esports federations or the motorsport bodies?" 这是电竞、赛车运动，还是一个还没名字的新东西？你希望谁来认证你们：电竞协会还是汽摩联？ Why: Western business audiences love a category-creation story. Their answer sets the frame for the whole episode.
7. **Who wins.** "Who wins: sim racers, RC racers, or people who have driven on a real track? Has anyone surprised you?" 谁赢得多：模拟器玩家、遥控车玩家，还是真赛道车手？有没有让你意外的人？ Why: human stories, and a quiet answer to whether skill transfers.
8. **The audience.** "Who watched the last event, how many, and what did people write in the comments that you did not expect?" 上一场谁在看、多少人、评论里有什么是你没想到的？ Why: real numbers and a glimpse of the community.
9. **The Stockholm driver.** "If a driver in Stockholm wanted to race in your next final, what would you need: technically, legally, commercially?" 如果一个斯德哥尔摩的车手想参加你们下一场决赛，你们需要什么：技术上、法律上、商业上？ Why: makes the international ambition concrete and puts you in the frame as the bridge.
10. **Robots under a racing brand.** "Why run robot competitions under a racing brand? What does a robot race teach you that a car race cannot?" 为什么在一个赛车品牌下做机器人比赛？机器人比赛能教你什么，赛车教不了？ Why: the bridge between the two halves of the episode, in their words.
11. **Money.** "Where does the money come from today: tickets, sponsors, the telecom partners, technology licensing? Which one do you want it to be in three years?" 现在的钱从哪来：门票、赞助、运营商、技术授权？三年后你希望是哪一个？ Why: the business model without an accountant's spreadsheet. Follow up only if they volunteer numbers.
12. **The sceptic, asked kindly.** "A sceptic in Europe will say: it is RC cars with a 5G sticker. What is your answer?" 欧洲的怀疑者会说：不就是贴了 5G 标签的遥控车吗。你怎么回答？ Why: The Circuit always asks the critic's question. Asking it first takes the sting out of it in the comments.
13. **What changed quietly.** "What did you change after the last event that nobody noticed?" 上一场之后你们改了什么是没人注意到的？ Why: small question, true answer. Use it whenever an answer has gone to PR.
14. **2030.** "Describe the final in 2030. Where are the cars, where are the drivers, and who is commentating?" 描述 2030 年的总决赛：车在哪，车手在哪，谁在解说？ Why: the vision close. Concrete, not abstract.

## Interview 2: the broadcast lead (转播)

Goal: the contrast between what the driver sees and what the audience sees, the craft of making a new sport watchable, and the partnership opening for your studio. Twelve questions.

1. **The last 60 seconds.** "Walk me through the last sixty seconds before you go live. What are you looking at?" 直播前最后 60 秒，你在看什么？ Why: process under tension, the best way into a craft person.
2. **Two kinds of video.** "There are two kinds of video in this building: what the driver sees and what the audience sees. How different are they, and why?" 这个场馆里有两种视频：车手看到的和观众看到的。差别多大？为什么？ Why: this is the technical explanation told by a storyteller. Milliseconds versus seconds.
3. **Cameras.** "How many cameras, which ones are on the car, and which single shot could you not broadcast a race without?" 多少个机位，哪些在车上，哪一个镜头是没有它比赛就没法播的？ Why: gives your own crew the map of their feed.
4. **Douyin versus the stadium.** "What does a viewer on Douyin see that a person in the venue never sees?" 抖音上的观众能看到什么是现场观众永远看不到的？ Why: overlays, driver cam, telemetry. The answer is your graphics list.
5. **Show the number?** "Do you show the latency number to the audience? Why, or why not?" 你们给观众显示延迟数值吗？为什么？ Why: a storytelling decision that reveals how they think about their own technology. If they do not, suggest it on camera; it may become a feature.
6. **Best and worst.** "The best moment you have broadcast so far, and the one you wish you had not." 播过最好的一刻，和最希望没播出去的一刻。 Why: two stories, one question.
7. **The non-gamer.** "How do you make someone who does not play games care? Who is the main character of a race: the driver, the car or the engineer?" 怎么让不玩游戏的人也看得进去？一场比赛的主角是车手、车，还是工程师？ Why: the question every new sport has to answer. Their answer tells you whether they have one.
8. **Drive to Survive.** "Formula 1 took seventy years and a Netflix series to find a new audience. What is your Drive to Survive?" F1 花了七十年加上一部 Netflix 剧才找到新观众。你们的《疾速争胜》是什么？ Why: a reference every Western viewer knows. It also sets up your studio's role.
9. **One show or two.** "Same race, a viewer in Shanghai and a viewer in Stockholm. Do you produce one show or two? Commentary, language, pacing?" 同一场比赛，上海的观众和斯德哥尔摩的观众：一套节目还是两套？解说、语言、节奏？ Why: exposes whether they have thought about an international product.
10. **Who they copied.** "Which broadcast did you copy when you started, and where did you stop copying?" 你们开始时模仿了哪家的转播？从哪里开始不再模仿？ Why: craftspeople answer this honestly and it is always interesting.
11. **Numbers.** "Peak concurrent viewers, average watch time, and the share who watched to the end." 峰值同时在线、平均观看时长、看到最后的比例。 Why: three numbers that tell a Western media buyer whether this is real.
12. **The opening for you.** "What could a Western studio do with your feed that you cannot do yourselves?" 我们作为一个西方工作室，能用你们的信号做什么你们自己做不了的事？ Why: the partnership question, asked at the end when trust is highest. Ask it off camera as well.

## Interview 3: the CTO or technology lead (技术)

Goal: the number, where the milliseconds go, what they built versus bought, what happens when the link drops, and the data angle. This is the interview that gives the episode its credibility with engineers. Fifteen questions; ask the first six in order.

1. **Set the level.** "Explain the system to me as if I were the T-box engineer I used to be. What is inside the Ro-box, and what lives in the cloud?" 把我当成我以前做 T-box 的工程师来解释：Ro-box 里有什么，云端有什么？ Why: tells them you can follow, so they skip the marketing layer.
2. **The number.** "End to end, from the car's camera to the driver's eye and back to the steering: how many milliseconds? Best case, race-day typical, and the worst you can still race on." 端到端，从车上摄像头到车手的眼睛再回到转向：多少毫秒？最好的、比赛日典型的、最差还能跑的。 Why: the one number the whole episode hangs on. Get three values, not one.
3. **Where it goes.** "Where do those milliseconds go: encode, radio, transit, decode, screen? Which one did you fight hardest?" 这些毫秒花在哪：编码、空口、传输、解码、屏幕？哪一块你们花了最大力气？ Why: the answer is the block diagram, narrated.
4. **Across a border.** "Shanghai to Stockholm is about 160 milliseconds there and back on today's internet, double what you quote for the track. Have you driven across a border yet? What was the number, and what broke?" 上海到斯德哥尔摩，在今天的互联网上一个来回大概 160 毫秒，是你们赛道数字的两倍。你们跨境开过吗？数字是多少？什么坏了？ Why: the stunt and its limit. If they have not done it, the question plants the idea of doing it with you.
5. **Link loss.** "The link drops at sixty kilometres an hour on the straight. What happens in the next two hundred milliseconds, in order?" 直道上时速六十公里时断链了，接下来二百毫秒依次发生什么？ Why: safety told as a sequence. Also the robot safety story in miniature.
6. **Prediction.** "Does the driver see where the car is, or where it will be? If you predict, is that an assist the referee should know about?" 车手看到的是车现在的位置，还是将来的位置？如果你们做预测，裁判需要知道吗？ Why: links the engineering to the sport. Their answer decides whether fairness is a solved problem.
7. **Build or buy.** "Which parts did you build and which did you buy: the modem, the codec, the transport protocol, the control stack, the box itself?" 哪些是自研、哪些是外购：模组、编解码、传输协议、控制栈、盒子本身？ Why: the differentiation test. A good CTO answers this with pride and precision.
8. **The operator.** "Do you need the telecom operator's cooperation, private 5G, slicing, edge computing, or does this run on a home connection? Which one does the final run on?" 需不需要运营商配合，专网、切片、边缘计算，还是家庭宽带就行？决赛用的是哪种？ Why: decides whether this scales to a driver in Stockholm or stays a venue product.
9. **Car to robot.** "When the far end is a humanoid instead of a car, what changes in the stack? Hands, balance, more cameras, less speed?" 远端从赛车换成人形机器人时，技术栈里什么变了？手、平衡、更多摄像头、更低速度？ Why: the bridge, in engineering terms.
10. **The surprise.** "After the first cross-country robot run, what did the operator on the other end tell you that you did not expect?" 第一次跨国机器人跑完后，另一端的操作员告诉你什么是你没想到的？ Why: a story engineers only tell when asked exactly this way.
11. **Data.** "Every race produces video, control inputs and car state on the same timestamps. Is that a product? Who wants it?" 每场比赛都产生同步时间戳的视频、控制输入和车辆状态。这是产品吗？谁要？ Why: the embodied-AI data angle, which is the business the West is currently paying for.
12. **Security.** "Someone on the internet wants to take over a car mid-race. How do you stop them?" 有人想在比赛中从互联网接管一辆车。你怎么拦？ Why: the question a Western CTO would ask first.
13. **Honest limits.** "What can this not do yet that people assume it can?" 大家以为能做而其实还做不到的是什么？ Why: credibility. The admission is the clip engineers will respect.
14. **For the camera.** "Can you put the latency on the screen, live, while I drive a lap?" 能不能在我开一圈的时候把延迟实时显示在屏幕上？ Why: the demonstration beat of the episode. Ask for it in the pre-interview so it is ready.
15. **The call.** "If Ericsson's or Huawei's 5G team called tomorrow, what would you ask them for?" 如果爱立信或华为的 5G 团队明天打电话来，你会要什么？ Why: a wish list is a roadmap, and Ericsson is in your country.

## Interview 4: a driver and a robot operator

Goal: the body, not the technology. These are the clips that travel. Pick the driver who lost the final, not the one who won; losers tell better stories. Keep each interview under 20 minutes.

**The driver.**

1. **In the body.** "What does the delay feel like? Describe it in your body, not in numbers." 延迟是什么感觉？用身体描述，不要用数字。 Why: the personal-habit clip the episode needs.
2. **Where you sat.** "Where were you sitting when you lost the final? What was on the wall in front of you?" 你输掉决赛的时候坐在哪？你面前的墙上有什么？ Why: place and detail make it a story.
3. **Three kinds of driving.** "You have raced in a game, with a handheld transmitter, and like this. Rank them by how hard they are, and by how real they feel." 你在游戏里开过、用手持遥控器开过、也这样开过。按难度排个序，再按真实感排个序。 Why: the differentiation question, answered by the only person who can.
4. **The first crash.** "Your first crash in one of these: did you feel anything? What did you do in the next second?" 你第一次在这种车上撝车：有感觉吗？下一秒你做了什么？ Why: pairs with the cold open.
5. **Last lap.** "What do you do differently on the last lap? Does the delay change how you brake?" 最后一圈你会做什么不同的事？延迟会改变你刹车的方式吗？ Why: craft detail; engineers and racers both lean in.
6. **Training.** "How do you train for this? Is there anything you do that a sim racer would laugh at?" 你怎么训练？有没有什么做法是模拟器玩家会笑话的？ Why: humour, and a window into the skill.
7. **To a European.** "A sim racer in Europe thinks this would be easy for them. What would you tell them?" 欧洲的模拟器车手觉得这对他们很容易。你会对他们说什么？ Why: a challenge across the border is a hook in itself.

**The robot operator.** If the robot programme is still at demo stage, interview whoever ran the WAIC or test runs; say so in the cut.

1. **The set-up.** "What are you controlling, from where, and how far away is it?" 你在控制什么，从哪里，离你多远？ Why: orientation for the viewer in one answer.
2. **Hardest thing.** "What is the hardest thing a robot does by remote: walking, picking something up, or turning around?" 机器人遥控最难的是什么：走路、拿东西，还是转身？ Why: a non-obvious answer, usually.
3. **The unexpected.** "Has the robot ever done something you did not ask it to?" 机器人有没有做过你没让它做的事？ Why: story, and a glimpse of where autonomy and remote control meet.
4. **When it falls.** "When the robot falls, what do you feel?" 机器人摔倒的时候，你有什么感觉？ Why: the human clip.
5. **What are you.** "Do you think of yourself as a gamer, a pilot, or a robot trainer?" 你觉得自己是玩家、飞行员，还是机器人训练师？ Why: the answer names the new job this creates.
6. **The car question.** "You have driven the race car too. Which is harder, and why?" 你也开过赛车。哪个更难，为什么？ Why: the bridge again, said by a practitioner.

## Trending-topic questions: memory chips, AI compute and the hooks of the moment

Memory chips (存储芯片: DRAM, HBM, NAND) are the hottest business story in the world this year, and they are a detour for this company unless each question lands back on their car, their cockpit or their data. So use these as bridges, not as a segment: at most three per sit-down, the memory questions to the CTO and the founder, the rest wherever they fit. A Western viewer who knows nothing about remote racing knows about the RAM shortage and about Nvidia; these questions let them in.

**The facts behind the questions, as reported in October 2026; verify on the day.** SK Hynix posted a first-quarter operating margin near 72% and said high-end memory stays tight for about three more years ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/sk-hynix-forecasts-prolonged-chip-supercycle-with-supply-constraints-to-persist-3-more-years/12556961)). Samsung has reportedly raised memory prices by up to 60% since September as AI data centres absorb supply ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/samsung-raises-memory-chip-prices-by-up-to-60-percent-since-september-according-to-reports-ai-data-center-build-out-strangles-supply)). Consumer DDR5 and NAND prices have roughly doubled, and HBM, DRAM and NAND are described as sold out for 2026 ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hardware/memory/were-facing-what-has-never-happened-before-hdd-dram-hbm-nand-all-in-severe-shortage-in-2026-says-silicon-motions-ceo-as-memory-prices-continue-to-soar)). CXMT (长鑫) raised about 57.9 billion yuan in its Shanghai listing in July 2026, closed its first day up 466%, and named no HBM project in its prospectus ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cxmt-closes-up-466-percent-in-shanghai-debut-with-no-hbm-project-in-its-ipo-prospectus)). YMTC (长江存储) is reported at about 13% of global NAND and preparing its own IPO ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20260606/chinese-memory-firms-edge-closer-to-challenging-koreas-chip-giants)).

**Memory chips, to the CTO.**

1. **The bill of materials.** "Samsung has reportedly raised memory prices by up to 60% since September, and DRAM is sold out for the year. Your cockpits, your edge servers and your Ro-boxes all carry memory. What has the AI boom done to the cost of building one car and one cockpit?"
    三星据报道自 9 月以来把存储价格提高了最多 60%，DRAM 今年已经全部售罄。你们的座舱、边缘服务器、Ro-box 都用存储。AI 热潮让造一辆车加一个座舱的成本变了多少？
    Why: puts the world's hottest topic on their factory floor. A Western audience understands a bill of materials.
2. **The queue.** "When SK Hynix and Samsung are selling every chip to Nvidia's customers, where does a Shanghai sports-technology company stand in the queue? Do you buy Chinese memory, CXMT or YMTC, and does it work?"
    当 SK 海力士和三星把每一颗芯片都卖给英伟达的客户时，一家上海的科技体育公司在队伍里排在哪？你们买国产存储吗，长鑫或长江？好用吗？
    Why: China's chip self-sufficiency told from a buyer's seat, which is the version nobody films. CXMT's 466% debut makes it current.
3. **The data business meets the storage bill.** "Every race produces synchronised video, control inputs and car state. Storage is exactly the thing that got expensive this year. Is your data a product, or a cost?"
    每场比赛都产生同步的视频、控制输入和车辆状态。今年变贵的恰恰是存储。你们的数据是产品，还是成本？
    Why: ties the embodied-AI data story to the shortage and forces a real answer on unit economics.

**Memory chips, to the founder.**

4. **The three-year squeeze.** "The memory makers say supply stays tight for three more years. If hardware gets dearer every quarter, do you end up owning more cars or fewer, and do drivers start bringing their own cockpits?"
    存储厂商说供应还要紧三年。如果硬件每个季度都更贵，你们最后是持有更多车还是更少车？车手会不会开始自带座舱？
    Why: a strategy question with a visible consequence. The answer reveals whether they go asset-light or asset-heavy.

**AI compute and physical AI, to either.**

5. **Nvidia's story.** "Nvidia calls this the decade of physical AI, and Jensen Huang says the next wave is robots. Are you a customer of that story or a competitor to it, since your robots have a human inside?"
    英伟达把这十年叫作“物理 AI”，黄仁勋说下一波是机器人。你们是这个故事的客户，还是它的竞争者？毕竟你们的机器人里有一个人。
    Why: a name every Western viewer knows, and it positions them against the autonomy narrative in one answer.
6. **The DeepSeek move.** "DeepSeek proved China can do more with fewer chips. Is there a DeepSeek move in teleoperation: more with less bandwidth, cheaper hardware, smaller models?"
    DeepSeek 证明了中国能用更少的芯片做更多的事。遥操作里有没有一个 DeepSeek 式的做法：更少带宽、更便宜的硬件、更小的模型？
    Why: the most recognisable China-AI reference in the West; it invites the frugal-engineering story Chinese founders tell well.
7. **Humanoid season.** "Unitree is listed, and Beijing ran 666 teams of humanoids in August. Which humanoid will be on your track first, and will it be driven from another country?"
    宇树上市了，北京 8 月办了 666 支队伍的人形机器人运动会。哪家的人形机器人会先出现在你们的赛道上？会从另一个国家操控吗？
    Why: dated and concrete. Confirm Unitree's listing status on the day; it was widely reported as in process in 2026.
8. **The box and the border.** "Chips are the front line of the US-China contest. Is there anything in your box you could not buy tomorrow if the rules changed, and what would you do?"
    芯片是中美竞争的前线。你们盒子里有没有哪个东西明天规则一变就买不到？那你们怎么办？
    Why: the geopolitical question asked as an engineering question, which keeps it answerable and keeps them comfortable.
9. **What 5G was for.** "China Mobile is selling 5G-A now. Do you need it, or is this the first use case that makes an ordinary person feel why 5G existed?"
    中国移动在推 5G-A。你们需要它吗？还是说这是第一个让普通人感受到 5G 为什么存在的应用？
    Why: the telecom hook, and the line an Ericsson engineer will quote.

**Rapid-fire additions.**

- Nvidia or Huawei: whose chips are in the box? 英伟达还是华为：盒子里是谁的芯片？
- If DRAM doubles again, what do you cut first? 如果 DRAM 再涨一倍，你先砍什么？
- The memory shortage, in one word. 用一个词形容存储短缺。

## Rapid-fire, closers, and the lines you must get

**Rapid-fire, for every interviewee, at the end, fast.** The Circuit closes its long episodes this way. Say "short answers, first thing that comes to mind" and keep the camera rolling.

- Real car or simulator: which is harder? 真车还是模拟器，哪个更难？
- Your latency, in one word. 用一个词形容你们的延迟。
- Initial D or Drive to Survive? 《头文字 D》还是《疾速争胜》？
- Who in the world would you most like to race? 你最想和世界上谁比一场？
- Would you put your champion against an AI driver, the way Abu Dhabi does? 你会让你们的冠军和 AI 车手比吗，像阿布扎比那样？
- Five years from now, what gets driven remotely that nobody expects? 五年后，什么东西会被远程驾驶是没人预料到的？
- Finish the sentence: "People think we are an RC car company, but..." 接下去：“大家以为我们是遥控车公司，但其实…”
- One number everyone should remember from today. 今天所有人都应该记住的一个数字。
- Motion seat: does it make you faster, or just happier? 动感座椅：让你更快，还是只是更开心？

**Closers, one per sit-down, asked last and slowly.**

- Founder: "If you take away the first-time novelty, why does anyone come back?" 如果去掉第一次的新鲜感，人为什么还会回来？
- CTO: "What is the one thing that, if it broke on race day, would end the company?" 比赛日如果有一件事坏了会让公司完蛋，是什么？
- Broadcast lead: "What moment are you still waiting to capture?" 你还在等待捕捉的是哪一个瞬间？
- Driver: "What do you tell your parents you do?" 你跟父母说你是做什么的？

**The lines you must get on camera, whatever else happens.** Check them off during the shoot. If a line is missing at wrap, go back and ask again.

- [ ] The CTO says the end-to-end number, in milliseconds, in a full sentence.
- [ ] The founder defines the sport in one sentence, in their own words.
- [ ] The broadcast lead says what the audience sees that the driver does not.
- [ ] A driver describes the delay in their body.
- [ ] Someone says, unprompted or prompted, that the cars are the test and the robots are the business. Let them find their own words; do not feed the line.
- [ ] The sceptic's question and an answer to it.
- [ ] The honest-limits admission from the CTO.
- [ ] The "first in China" claim with its qualifier, said by them, not by you.
- [ ] Your lap, with the counter on screen, start to finish, including the crash if there is one.

**If you get only ten minutes with the founder.** Ask, in this order: origin with a failure (1), one sentence two audiences (2), the crash (4), fairness (5), the sceptic (12), the novelty closer. Six questions, ten minutes, enough for a cut.
