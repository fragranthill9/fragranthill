Five workflows, three lanes, one calendar.

Each pillar runs as a swimlane: what Lan does, what AI agents do, what a partner or contractor does. The rule is simple. Humans own the thesis, the relationship and the price. Agents own research, production, distribution and follow-up. Partners own what they already have: data, cohorts, venues, invoicing in China. Nothing here needs a full-time hire in year one.

Shared core · Relationship graph

One CRM (Attio or HubSpot) holds every guest, sponsor, member, founder and investor with a pillar tag. Agents read and write it; Lan sees one pipeline.

Shared core · Production pipeline

Riverside → Descript → OpusClip → HeyGen → scheduled posts via n8n. Lean stack runs at roughly $350–600 a month, rising to $900–1,400 with agents and API usage.

Shared core · Back office

Swedish AB invoices Europe. A Chinese partner entity issues fapiao and remits net of its fee, which avoids the 6% withholding on services paid to a foreign company.

P1 · Editorial

One studio day, six channels, two languages.

Six months of recording produced no audience because every piece was a one-off: a new guest, a hired crew, one channel, no theme. The fix is a repeatable line, not more effort.

Cadence

Weekly: Monday plan, Tuesday record two guests, Thursday the AI pipeline delivers cuts for review, Friday publish everywhere. Monthly: one sponsor recap. Quarterly: one season.

Targets and unit economics

Month 6: 2,000 email subscribers, 3,000 LinkedIn, 1,500 YouTube, first season sponsor. Month 12: 6,000 / 10,000 / 5,000 and €500–1,000 per newsletter slot.

P2 · Advisory

Turn partner data and the founder's seat into paid answers.

The partner holds data, reports, analysis and a founder network that currently earn nothing in Europe. A Swedish operator with an automotive and sensing background is the missing translator.

Cadence

Two advisory days a week maximum, so Editorial keeps its rhythm. Quarterly: one co-branded report sold under licence.

Targets and unit economics

Day rate €1,500–3,000. Target by month 12: two retainers at €4–6k a month plus one report licence a quarter.

P3 · Community

From audience to a vetted room, in two tiers.

Memberships only work with trust already built. This pillar starts after Editorial has 2,000 real subscribers and Live has filled one room.

Cadence

Monthly salon (hosted on a Live stage), quarterly course, one study trip a year in each direction.

Targets and unit economics

Base tier €400 a year, vetted club €4,000–8,000 a year. Month 12: 80 base members, 15 club members. Month 24: 300 and 50.

P4 · Live & Venues · Encore

From stage access to sponsor cash, in one quarter.

This is the entry pillar. It is the only line that sells on access and association rather than follower counts, so it can close before the audience exists.

Cadence

One flagship tour a year, four executive salons a year on the same stages, one sponsor dinner per city.

Targets and unit economics

Year 1: one presenting partner and two official partners. Fragrant Hill margin 20–30% of gross sponsorship plus production fees.

P5 · Acceleration

From deal flow to carry, without running a fund.

Deal flow is a by-product of the other four pillars. Economics sit in the partner's fund and in programme fees, so this pillar carries no fixed cost.

Cadence

One cohort a year of 6–8 companies, timed with the Stockholm salon and a Live stage.

Targets and unit economics

Programme fee €15–25k per company, scout fee 2–5% of round, carry negotiated with the partner. First cohort in year 2.

Operating calendar

WeeklyMonday: plan and pipeline review (30 min). Tuesday: record two guests. Thursday: review AI cuts, approve. Friday: publish in six channels, send sponsor follow-ups.
MonthlyOne salon on a Live stage (60–100 guests, 2–3 sponsors). One sponsor recap. One member digest. One P&L by pillar.
QuarterlyOne editorial season closes and the next opens. One co-branded report. One executive dinner per market. Board-style review with the partner.
YearlyOne flagship tour (Encore). One study trip in each direction. One cohort of founders. One "Requests for Companies" list published.
