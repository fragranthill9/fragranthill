A proposal for collaboration

The house that tells the story of the people building machines.

Fragrant Hill Studio proposes a partnership that turns assets both sides already hold, a stage, a network, data and a voice, into cash within one quarter and into a recurring business within two years. This memo gives the answer first, then the evidence, then the ask. It is written to be read in twelve minutes, in the order of the chapters below.

1 · The answer

Start with the one product that pays before an audience exists, then build the house around it.

We propose a two-step partnership. Step one is Encore: a presenting-partner programme for a world-class pianist's 2027 European tour, sold to automotive, robotics and technology brands with 2027 plans in Europe. Fragrant Hill holds the stage and the artist relationship; the partner holds the introductions. Sponsors pay 50% on signature. The base case closes €220k of sponsorship within 60 days of launch and earns the partnership €64k; the full ladder is €1.1M gross and €332k to the partnership over 2027.

Step two is the operating system behind it. Five pillars, one editorial core, one relationship graph, run by one founder, part-time contractors and six AI agents. Encore provides the cash and the content; a focused interview series builds the audience; advisory monetises the partner's data in Europe; a vetted club and an acceleration programme follow once trust exists. The three-year planning case reaches €1.8M revenue with three to four people, in line with the best AI-native media houses today.

What we ask is twenty warm introductions within three weeks, one named champion, and a go decision by 15 November 2026. What the partner gets is a share of every sponsorship it introduces, a European distribution and advisory channel for its data and founders, and first call on the next three Encore editions.

2 · What each side holds

Neither side can do this alone, and neither needs to fund the other.

Fragrant Hill brings a Swedish editorial house with a bilingual production base, a founder with a VP seat in a 3D-sensing company and fifteen years in automotive and dealer networks, a completed long-form interview with the artist, and a channel to host celebrity concerts in Europe. Six months of early publishing on WeChat taught us two things: production costs money when every piece is a one-off, and content with no theme builds no audience. Both lessons are designed into the system in chapter 4.

The partner brings proprietary data, industry reports and analysis on a fast-growing hardware sector, a community of several hundred founders, relationships with investors and industrial groups, and the introductions into automotive and robotics brands that Encore needs. Those assets earn little in Europe today because there is no European voice, channel or operator attached to them. Fragrant Hill is that operator.

The stage is ours. The doors are the partner's. The revenue is shared on what closes, so neither side carries the other.
3 · What the best media businesses teach

The money is never in the content. It is in the room the content opens.

We benchmarked twenty-four media businesses that turned a voice into profit, from celebrity houses and creator channels to venture-capital media, premium journalism, data platforms, event companies and paid clubs, in the United States, Europe and China. Four numbers carry the argument.

50–60%

of Semafor's revenue is event sponsorship. First profitable year in 2025 on $40M revenue, built on a free invite-only CEO newsletter.

<5%

of SemiAnalysis revenue comes from its newsletter. Research models and consulting carry a business heading past $100M.

90%

of a Chinese business pundit's year-end show revenue is sponsorship. The same tentpole logic funds the New Year speech watched by 45 million people.

Six rules follow. One ritual, not random content. Sell the room before the reach. Build a SemiAnalysis, not a Stratechery: in a B2B niche the newsletter is marketing and the profit is in data and advisory priced from €20k. Two tiers for everything. Media as deal flow is real but comes last. Keep overhead near zero: the houses that cut staff in 2024–2026 were the ones that hired for production. The full benchmark, with a revenue-mix chart and a map of where each model lives, is Document 1.

4 · The design

One editorial core, five product lines, one flywheel.

Fragrant Hill is built as a single operating system rather than five businesses. Four inputs we already hold feed one core: an editorial engine with a single theme, a relationship graph, a production pipeline, a commercial desk and a back office. The core runs five product pillars. The sequence is deliberate: Live first because it sells on access rather than followers, Editorial second with one theme, Community and Advisory third once trust exists, Acceleration last as the by-product of being the house everyone talks to.

5 · How it runs

Humans own the thesis, the relationship and the price. Agents own everything repeatable.

Each pillar runs as a swimlane with three lanes: the founder, AI agents, and partners or contractors. One studio day feeds six channels in two languages through a transcription, cutting, dubbing and scheduling pipeline that costs roughly $350–600 a month. Advisory work caps at two days a week so the editorial rhythm holds. Sponsorship sales are drafted by an agent and closed by a human. Finance, invoicing and the China entity sit with partners. Year-one headcount is one founder, about 1.5 contractor FTE, and six agent roles built on the skills already written for this house: content pipeline, sponsorship proposal, design system, English coaching.

Weekly

Monday plan. Tuesday record two guests. Thursday review AI cuts. Friday publish in six channels and send sponsor follow-ups.

Monthly and quarterly

One salon on a Live stage with 2–3 sponsors. One sponsor recap, one member digest, one P&L by pillar. Each quarter a season closes and a co-branded report ships.

Yearly

One flagship tour. One study trip in each direction. One founder cohort. One published "Requests for Companies" list, the YC device adapted to Nordic robotics.

The five workflow diagrams, the stack map of skills, connectors and agents, and the operating calendar are Document 3.

6 · The first product

Encore: a presenting-partner programme for a 2027 European tour, first cash in sixty days.

The property. A pianist with sold-out halls across Europe and a claimed 16–20 million social following, a current major-label album, an autumn 2026 European run and listed 2027 dates in London, Hamburg and Bonn. No whole-tour presenting partner has ever been announced for this artist. A Nordic leg is open, and November is when 2027 marketing budgets are set.

The buyers. European premium car makers have sponsored classical music for decades. Chinese automotive, robotics and consumer-tech brands entering Europe have not used the playbook; their European marketing went to football. A brand launching in the Nordics needs a room where dealers, fleet buyers, journalists and officials want to be. A concert hall is that room.

Ladder: one presenting partner at €450k, three category-exclusive official partners at €150k, six hospitality partners at €35k. Prices anchor on the only disclosed classical-music fee at this scale, on six-figure festival and radio deals, and on the artist's own earlier personal sponsorship.

The honest caveat. Six-figure sponsorships normally take 9–18 months to close. Sixty days works only with warm introductions, the Q4 budget window and deposit terms. The base case needs three signatures, not ten, and if fewer than five term sheets are live by week six we extend the window and say so. The artist's automotive history means category availability is confirmed in writing before any car brand is pitched; if automotive is closed, the presenting tier goes to robotics and consumer technology.
